# LM-Verify ⚖️

### Automated Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011 Compliance Checking Platform

LM-Verify is an AI-assisted compliance verification platform designed for enforcement officers working under the **Legal Metrology Act, 2009** and the **Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011**.

The system allows an officer to capture package images, extract statutory declarations, evaluate them against a versioned rule pack, calculate applicable Rule 32 penalty exposure, route uncertain findings for human review, and generate verifiable compliance reports.

> **Core Principle:** The vision layer reads and measures. It never decides compliance.  
> Compliance verdicts are generated by deterministic rule functions operating on a versioned JSON rule pack.

---

## 📌 Problem Statement

Manual inspection of packaged commodities requires officers to verify several declarations and regulatory requirements on product packaging.

These include:

- Manufacturer / packer / importer details
- Common or generic name
- Net quantity
- Month and year of packing
- Retail Sale Price (MRP)
- Consumer care details
- Correct units
- Language requirements
- Legibility and prominence
- Applicable category-specific requirements

Manual verification can be repetitive and difficult to reproduce consistently.

LM-Verify provides a structured workflow that combines image extraction, deterministic rule evaluation, human adjudication, and evidence-preserving reporting.

---

## 💡 Solution

LM-Verify follows the workflow:

```text
Package Image
     ↓
AI-assisted Extraction
     ↓
Structured Declarations
     ↓
Category & Scope Resolution
     ↓
Deterministic Rule Engine
     ↓
Compliance Results
     ↓
Human Review / Adjudication
     ↓
Rule 32 Exposure
     ↓
Evidentiary Report
     ↓
SHA-256 + QR Verification
```

The AI model is used to identify and structure information printed on the package. The final compliance decision is made separately by the rule engine.

# ⭐ Key Features

## 1. Automated Declaration Extraction

LM-Verify extracts important statutory declarations from package images, including:

- Manufacturer / packer / importer details
- Common or generic product name
- Net quantity
- Month and year of packing
- Retail Sale Price / MRP
- Consumer care information

The extracted information is retained both as the original printed text and in normalized form for rule evaluation.

---

## 2. Deterministic Rule Engine

Compliance decisions are not made directly by the AI model.

Rules are stored in a versioned JSON rule pack:

```text
backend/src/data/rulePack_v1.json
```

The rule engine evaluates extracted information using deterministic functions.

This allows the regulatory logic to be:

- Readable
- Versioned
- Auditable
- Reproducible
- Independently updated from the vision model

---

## 3. Category & Scope Resolution

Before evaluating compliance, the system determines whether the package falls within the applicable regulatory scope.

The project contains category-specific information for **26 product categories**.

The system also handles relevant exemptions and category-specific regulatory differences.

---

## 4. Multi-Face Package Analysis

LM-Verify can process multiple faces of the same package.

This helps capture information that may appear on:

- Front panel
- Back panel
- Side panel

If different package faces contain conflicting declarations, both readings are preserved instead of silently selecting one.

Example:

```text
Front → Net Quantity: 500 g
Back  → Net Quantity: 450 g

        ↓

Conflict detected
        ↓
Officer Review
```

---

## 5. Human-in-the-Loop Review

The system does not force an automated decision when the available evidence is insufficient.

Rule results can be:

```text
PASS
FAIL
REVIEW
NOT_APPLICABLE
NOT_ASSESSED
```

Uncertain findings are routed to an officer for review.

Senior Inspectors and Controllers can override individual rule results with a recorded reason.

---

## 6. Rule 32 Penalty Exposure

Penalty exposure is calculated from applicable rule violations.

Only:

```text
FAIL
```

contributes to Rule 32 exposure.

A `REVIEW` result does not automatically create a penalty.

After an officer override, the exposure is recalculated using the effective verdicts.

---

## 7. Evidentiary Reports

After adjudication, the system can generate reports in:

```text
PDF
DOCX
XLSX
JSON
```

The underlying report data is stored as structured JSON.

At issue time:

```text
Report Payload
     ↓
SHA-256 Hash
     ↓
QR Token
     ↓
Verifiable Report
```

Reports can be re-rendered from the stored payload and verified through the QR endpoint.

# 📏 Physical Measurement Pipeline

The legacy vision pipeline supports physical measurements required by geometry-related rules.

The measurement workflow is:

```text
OCR Localization
      ↓
Crop Original Image
      ↓
4× Upscaling
      ↓
Sauvola Binarization
      ↓
Connected Components
      ↓
Glyph Filtering
      ↓
Median Glyph Height
      ↓
mm/px Calibration
      ↓
Physical Measurement
```

The system intentionally avoids measuring glyph height directly from OCR bounding boxes because OCR boxes include surrounding padding and character-specific variation.

# 🚦 Verdict System

Each individual rule can return:

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `PASS` | Requirement is satisfied |
| `FAIL` | Contravention detected |
| `REVIEW` | Human review required |
| `NOT_APPLICABLE` | Rule does not apply |
| `NOT_ASSESSED` | Required measurement/check is not performed |

The overall inspection status is derived from these results.

```text
No FAIL + No REVIEW + No NOT_ASSESSED
                    ↓
                COMPLIANT

At least one FAIL
                    ↓
             NON_COMPLIANT

No FAIL + REVIEW / NOT_ASSESSED
                    ↓
                 REVIEW
```

---

# 🎯 Confidence Gating

The system uses confidence levels to control whether a result can be asserted.

```text
HIGH
↓
Result can be recorded

MEDIUM
↓
Result becomes REVIEW
↓
Officer must verify

LOW
↓
No unsupported assertion
↓
Inspection routed for review
```

Measurements close to statutory thresholds are downgraded to MEDIUM confidence rather than being treated as definite violations.

##  Conclusion

LM-Verify transforms packaged-commodity inspection from a largely manual process into a structured, traceable and evidence-backed workflow.

The system combines AI-assisted extraction with deterministic statutory evaluation. The vision layer is responsible for reading and measuring package information, while the compliance decision is made by versioned rule functions rather than by a model. This separation makes the resulting verdicts reproducible, explainable and easier to audit.

The platform also handles uncertainty explicitly. When an image cannot support a reliable measurement, the system does not guess. Instead, it marks the relevant check as `NOT_ASSESSED` or routes it for human review. Senior officers can override findings with a recorded reason, preserving the human-in-the-loop nature of enforcement.

From package capture to rule evaluation, adjudication and hash-sealed report generation, LM-Verify provides a complete inspection lifecycle with authentication, RBAC, audit logging and verifiable digital evidence.

The core principle of LM-Verify is simple:

> **AI reads. Deterministic rules decide. Humans adjudicate. Evidence remains verifiable.**
